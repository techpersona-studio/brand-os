## 2. Visual direction recommendation

### Primary direction

**Editorial / Human**

### Secondary influence

**Cutting-edge / Innovative**

### Direction mix

* Primary %: 65%
* Secondary %: 35%

### Why this direction

* The entire brand is experience-led — bar, food, karaoke. Editorial/Human holds warmth, intimacy, and Vietnamese identity as the dominant register.
* The existing visual assets (13 professional food photos, dark moody interior, red lanterns, Vietnamese street mural) are editorial assets, not product marketing assets. The direction must match what already exists.
* Trust burden is medium, earned through atmosphere and authenticity — not enterprise credibility or startup momentum. Enterprise direction would read as stiff; Cutting-edge alone reads as tech.
* The 18–35 bar crowd responds to places that feel real, alive, and specific — not dashboard-clean or corporately stable.
* Voice is conversational and invite-forward. Visual language must match.
* Client confirmed: "Vietnamese vibe + nightlife energy" — Cutting-edge secondary is raised to 35% and runs throughout the full system, not confined to event blocks. Nightlife energy lives in contrast levels, heading weight, CTA assertiveness, and section pacing — not just the karaoke slab.

### Must avoid

1. **Generic Vietnamese takeout UI** — menus listed in plain tables, no atmosphere, no photography; looks like a delivery aggregator page
2. **Bright white restaurant site** — clinical and sterile; destroys the dark moody identity that the interior photos establish
3. **Fine dining minimalism** — too precious and slow-paced; alienates the 18–35 bar crowd who want energy, not reverence
4. **SaaS-style card grid layout** — icon-feature tiles, dashboard chrome, metric cards; wrong domain signal entirely
5. **Oversaturated chinoiserie "Asian restaurant" red** — flat #FF0000-adjacent red with gold borders reads as takeout signage, not nightlife bar
6. **Generic stock photography** — smiling couples at restaurant tables, posed groups laughing; breaks trust with food-first customers who want to see the real space
7. **TripAdvisor-style review aggregation layout** — commoditizes the brand, reduces a bar-restaurant to a ratings widget
8. **App-style mobile structure with bottom navigation** — wrong interaction model; this is a destination, not a utility app

## 4. Color direction

### Primary color logic

* Recommended anchor color family: Deep Vietnamese crimson
* Why: Red dominates the existing interior (red hanging lanterns, wall art). Red also signals occasion energy, bar life, and celebration — it is both culturally grounded (Vietnamese cultural red = luck, occasion) and functionally correct (CTA color for a nightlife-adjacent brand).
* Domain signal: Vietnamese cultural red + bar/nightlife energy
* Emotional role: Occasion trigger, CTA anchor, cultural identity marker

### Base color logic

* Base: Dark
* Neutral temperature: Warm
* Contrast level: High — dark base requires high contrast for readability; high contrast also serves the nightlife atmosphere

### Accent logic

* Accent usage: Controlled
* Accent role: CTA / Event highlights / Occasion energy markers
* Accent caution:
  * Never use as a large background wash — degrades the moody atmosphere
  * Never apply to body copy — too loud, reduces legibility
  * Should appear as a pulse point (button, event chip, proof badge), not a dominant color field

### Logo observation — locked from visual review

The actual Quan Pho logo is a gold circle badge with deep crimson type on a warm gold background. The neon green noted in the Business Persona is physical in-store signage only — it does not appear in the logo and does not carry into the digital system. This directly confirms and locks the warm gold + crimson palette with no green.

### Palette recommendation

* Neutral direction: Near-black with warm brown undertone — base `#0E0B09`, surface `#1C1510`, raised surface `#261C14`
* Anchor direction: Deep Vietnamese crimson — `#B5202B`; hover `#C92535`; wash `#2A1015`
* Secondary direction: Warm amber-gold (confirmed by logo badge) — `#D4A030`; warm variant `#E5B843` (food warmth, lighting warmth, logo badge echo, secondary emphasis)
* Accent direction: None — no green in the digital system

### Text colors

* Text primary: Warm off-white — `#F2EBE0`
* Text secondary: Warm mid-tone — `#A89880`

### Usage ratio

* Neutrals (base + surfaces): 60%
* Anchor red: 20%
* Secondary gold: 20%
* Accent: None

### State color rule

* Default state colors acceptable: No
* Explanation: Generic browser-blue or UI-gray hover states would break the dark warm palette entirely. All interactive states must use the defined warm palette — crimson for primary actions, gold for secondary hover, off-white for text states.

### Color anti-patterns

Avoid:

* Pure white backgrounds — destroys the moody dark identity
* Tech blue or UI-gray interactive defaults — completely wrong domain signal
* All three warm accents (red + gold + green) at full saturation simultaneously — creates visual noise, not atmosphere
* Oversaturated "Asian restaurant red" (flat #CC0000, no depth) — looks like a takeout sign, not a bar
* Color blocking without a role — accent color as decoration rather than function

## 5. Typography direction

### Typeface strategy

Hybrid — editorial serif display + clean sans body

### Typography role split

* Primary reading font: **Be Vietnam Pro** — body copy, UI labels, navigation, button text (~85%)
* Accent / display font: **Fraunces** — large hero phrases, section headers, editorial signature moments (~15%)
* Script font: None at this stage

### Tone

Human / Editorial

### Density

Balanced — spacious enough for photography to breathe; not so loose that it loses bar energy

### Hierarchy

Strong — high contrast between Fraunces display weight and Be Vietnam Pro body creates legible hierarchy on dark backgrounds

### Heading behavior

* Sentence case (no title case inflation)
* Tight tracking at display sizes (Fraunces large headings)
* Bold weight for Fraunces hero phrases; medium-regular for section subheads in Be Vietnam Pro

### Body behavior

* Plain / readable — no editorial flourishes in body copy
* Reading tone: direct, short sentences matching the voice guidelines
* Default weight: Regular (400), medium (500) for emphasis

### Signature move

Fraunces italic at large display size (hero or section intro), warm off-white on near-black. Creates one editorial moment per section — intimate, specific, alive. Used once per section maximum.

### Typography anti-patterns

Avoid:

* Decorative serif everywhere — tips into fine dining territory
* All-caps body copy — loses warmth, hard to read on dark backgrounds
* Generic SaaS sans pairing (Roboto + Inter, Open Sans + Poppins) — no character for this brand
* Novelty fonts with Vietnamese-aesthetic markers (look performative and generic)
* Too many weight variants simultaneously — creates visual noise, not hierarchy
* Condensed all-caps for body text — illegible at small sizes on dark backgrounds

## 6. Layout rhythm

### Layout mode

Flow-based — sections feel continuous and atmospheric, not modular grid boxes

### Grid

Flexible — not a locked 12-column SaaS grid; sections breathe and shift based on content

### Rhythm

Alternating — image-dominant and text-dominant sections alternate to sustain energy and prevent monotony

### Density

Spacious — dark backgrounds need generous whitespace/darkspace to breathe; cramped sections on dark surfaces feel oppressive

### Section pacing

Energetic/Narrative — sections move at nightlife pace, not fine dining pace; each section earns the next while sustaining forward momentum

### Hero structure

text + product — moody bar or food photography as hero background or side panel; bold Fraunces headline; dual CTA (Order online + Book your party) visible above fold

### Common section patterns

1. Full-width narrative (hero, event announcements — Saturday karaoke slab)
2. Card grid (menu highlights, cocktail showcases — 3-column max on desktop)
3. Proof band (review strip — Google stars, short named quotes)
4. CTA band (party booking — full width, high contrast, direct action)
5. Event block (Saturday karaoke — specific time, recurring framing, action CTA)

### Mobile behavior

* What must remain true on mobile: Hero headline + dual CTA above fold; event block (karaoke night) visible within first scroll; food photography preserves moody atmosphere even when cropped for portrait
* What may simplify on mobile: Card grids collapse to single column; proof band becomes horizontally scrollable row

### Layout anti-patterns

Avoid:

* Bright white card-heavy layout that looks like a food delivery listing
* Sidebar navigation (wrong interaction model for a restaurant site)
* Infinite-scroll menu list without photography
* Cramped sections — on a dark background, spacing is atmosphere
* App-style bottom navigation bar

## 7. Component language

### Overall feel

Soft / Rounded — warm and inviting, not sharp-edged enterprise components

### Card language

Shadow-led on dark surfaces — cards should appear to glow slightly against the dark background with a warm shadow; subtle warm border on hover state

### Button language

* Primary: Filled, rounded rectangle, deep crimson — assertive, visible CTA
* Secondary: Outline, rounded rectangle, warm off-white — quiet alternate action
* Never: Pill shape (too dating-app); sharp square corners (too enterprise); ghost-only CTAs for primary actions (too quiet for party booking)

### Form language

Warm / High-trust — booking and inquiry forms must feel personal, not like a ticketing widget

### Navigation feel

Quiet — dark, slim navigation that does not compete with content; logo + 2–3 key links maximum visible above fold

### Proof modules

1. Review strip (short Google 5★ quotes, named reviewer)
2. Credential bar (Yelp 4.0★, Google rating, total review count)
3. Quote blocks (specific dish or experience call-outs)
4. Founder note — chef photo + short credential line ("Family-owned. Chef trained in Vietnam for X years.") — photo confirmed available, story details pending client follow-up
5. Service proof row (bar photo, food photo, karaoke setup — side by side with short labels)

### Signature component

**Event announcement slab** — full-width dark-surface block with large Fraunces italic heading ("Saturday Karaoke Nights"), bold Be Vietnam Pro time and date detail, single crimson CTA. Appears once on home page. Communicates: this is a real weekly event, not aspirational marketing copy.

### Component anti-patterns

Avoid:

* App navigation chrome (tab bars, bottom nav, slide-out menus that feel like mobile apps)
* Overdesigned floating cards with multiple layered shadows
* Icon-heavy feature rows (SaaS product pattern, wrong domain)
* Accordion menus as the primary content structure for food categories
* Generic restaurant reservation widgets (OpenTable/Resy embeds) that break brand visual consistency
* Mismatched component styles (soft rounded cards next to sharp-cornered buttons)

## 8. Imagery and art direction

### Photography direction

Environment-led + Product-led

### Subject priority

Place first (bar interior, lanterns, street mural, atmosphere), Product second (close food + cocktail detail)

### Mood

Warm, Intimate, Moody

### Lighting

Available ambient — pendant lights, neon sign glow, red lanterns, natural warm shadows. No hard studio flash on interior shots.

### Composition

* Close-up: food plating detail, cocktail poured, bar ingredients
* Wide: interior atmosphere overview, bar section with people (when available)
* No medium-formal posed shots of staff or customers

### Proof imagery

* What proof should appear visually: Real bar section (not a stock image), actual cocktails poured, actual karaoke setup, actual food plates matching menu descriptions
* What should never be fabricated: Before/after food quality comparisons, fake crowd shots, stock group photos presented as real customers

### Illustration / icon direction

Minimal — functional icons only (navigation, UI affordances); no decorative illustration; no custom Vietnamese cultural imagery unless provided by the client

### Imagery anti-patterns

Avoid:

* Generic stock smiles — couples at restaurant tables, posed groups laughing at nothing
* Phone mockups showing the ordering interface
* Overhead flat-lay food shots used in dark-themed sections (clashes with moody atmosphere)
* Cold commercial food photography on white backgrounds presented inside dark-themed layouts
* Stock bar images from non-Vietnamese contexts
* Excessive blur or bokeh filter masking food quality
* Cliché "dragon and lotus" Vietnamese cultural illustration

## 9. Motion and interaction

### Motion level

Restrained

### Motion role

Reveal + Hover

### Button interaction

Tactile — slight scale (1.02) or color shift on hover/press; no magnetic or physics-based motion

### Scroll behavior

Fade-up — sections reveal as user scrolls; no parallax; no complex sequenced band reveals

### Performance rule

* What should animate: Section entrance (fade-up, 300–400ms), button hover state, card glow on hover
* What should never animate: Photography (ruins composition), body text during reading, background elements, navigation bar

### Reduced motion rule

Required: Yes — must honor `prefers-reduced-motion`; disable all entrance animations when set

### Motion anti-patterns

Avoid:

* Parallax scrolling on photography — destroys food photography composition and reads as 2015 design
* Floating CTA widgets that follow the scroll position
* Entrance animations that delay above-fold content visibility
* Continuous animation loops (pulsing, spinning, looping) visible in the base state
* Page transition effects — add latency without brand value

## 10. Do / Don't rules

### Do

1. Lead every section with atmosphere — show the space before the menu
2. Name the Spicy Pho Ti-Ni and Saturday Karaoke 8PM in or immediately below the hero
3. Use Fraunces italic for single hero phrases: "Come out tonight." "Pho and cocktails on Route 1 North."
4. Keep the dark base dark — never soften near-black to gray to "modernize" it
5. Show dual CTA (Order online + Book your party) above fold on every screen size
6. Use real named Google review quotes — short, specific, tied to the experience
7. Show the physical bar section — at minimum one full-width photo of the actual bar
8. Let food photography breathe — give featured dishes full-width or near-full-width treatment
9. Treat "Saturday karaoke 8PM" as a recurring event announcement, not incidental copy in a sidebar

### Don't

1. Don't use white or near-white backgrounds in any primary section — it breaks the identity
2. Don't show a menu list without photography — this brand competes on atmosphere, not price
3. Don't use stock photography of any kind — the existing professional photos are sufficient
4. Don't apply fine dining fonts, pacing, or minimalism to sections that serve bar customers
5. Don't make party booking a secondary CTA buried below the fold
6. Don't embed a generic reservation widget that doesn't match the visual system
7. Don't repeat "authentic" without a specific dish, cocktail, or cultural reference next to it
8. Don't apply anchor red to body text or use it as a large background wash
9. Don't design desktop-first — the 18–35 bar audience browses on mobile before deciding to go out
