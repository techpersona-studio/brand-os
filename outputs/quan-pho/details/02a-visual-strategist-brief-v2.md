## 2. Visual direction recommendation

### Primary direction

**Editorial / Human — 70%**

### Secondary influence

**Cutting-edge / Innovative — 30%**

The secondary influence covers the Instagram-friendly, bar-forward, modern casual layer. It must remain weaker than the primary warmth and cultural specificity.

### Direction mix

* Primary %: 70%
* Secondary %: 30%

### Why this direction

* **Trust burden is medium:** The brand needs warmth and human storytelling to build credibility with 9 Yelp reviews. Editorial / Human does this better than a clean tech grid.
* **Audience psychology:** 18–35 bar crowd responds to atmosphere, FOMO, and social proof. Editorial imagery and moody dark tones create desire; cutting-edge energy keeps it from feeling nostalgic-only.
* **Buyer trigger is experiential:** The decision to go out is triggered by mood. The site must sell the feeling of being there — that requires immersive, editorial visual language.
* **The interior is already the design brief:** Yellow walls, red lanterns, street-scene mural, dark wood, neon sign — this is editorial/human material. The visual system reflects what already exists.
* **Bar legitimacy signaling:** The full bar and Spicy Pho Ti-Ni cocktail need to read as credible nightlife, not as an afterthought. A cutting-edge secondary layer adds the right energy.
* **Anti-traits eliminate the alternatives:** Enterprise / Stable would feel corporate and cold. A heavy lean into Cutting-edge / Innovative alone would strip the cultural identity that differentiates the brand.

### Must avoid

* **Bright white restaurant template with a Google-font food brand** — positions the brand as interchangeable with every suburban pho shop
* **Red-and-gold Chinese restaurant color scheme** — colors are shared, but the visual language must be distinctly Vietnamese street aesthetic, not Cantonese-formal
* **Delivery-app card layout (DoorDash/Uber Eats visual style)** — erases bar legitimacy and social energy positioning
* **Michelin or fine-dining editorial photography** — polished white-plate overhead shots without atmosphere feel mismatched for a late-night bar/karaoke crowd
* **SaaS-style CTA buttons and card metrics** — cold, corporate, destroys the warm-evening buyer trigger
* **Stock photo Vietnamese families eating pho** — directly contradicts the no-kids, 18–35, bar-crowd positioning
* **Neon-overloaded "Asian fusion bar" aesthetic** — tacky and generic; this brand's neon is specific (the QUÁN PHỞ sign), not decorative wallpaper

## 4. Color direction

### Primary color logic

* **Recommended anchor color family:** Warm golden-yellow
* **Why:** The dominant yellow walls are the strongest confirmed visual signal from both the Business Persona and the space description. Gold anchors warmth, energy, and cultural specificity without tipping into Chinese restaurant red-and-gold cliché when balanced against a dark base.
* **Domain signal:** Yellow-gold reads warmth + energy in the restaurant/hospitality category; not corporate, not cold
* **Emotional role:** Warmth, invitation, vibrancy — the visual equivalent of "come in tonight"

### Base color logic

* **Base:** Dark — confirmed by client
* **Neutral temperature:** Warm (brown undertones, not blue-gray)
* **Contrast level:** High — dark backgrounds require high contrast for legibility and atmosphere

### Accent logic

* **Accent usage:** Controlled
* **Accent role:** CTA emphasis (gold), cultural identity (crimson-red), proof highlights
* **Accent caution:**
  * Gold must never appear as a generic "luxury" shimmer — use flat and clean
  * Red must never dominate over gold — it is a secondary signal, not the primary brand color
  * Neon green (`#52E074`) is confirmed logo/brand mark only — does not appear in the digital palette

### Palette recommendation

* **Base:** Very dark warm near-black — `#181008`
* **Surface (cards/sections):** Dark warm brown — `#241810`
* **Neutral (text secondary, dividers):** Warm mid-tone brown — `#5C4232`
* **Text primary:** Warm cream off-white — `#F0E8D0`
* **Anchor:** Warm golden-yellow — `#F2B928`; hover `#E0A820`; wash `#F2B92812`
* **Secondary:** Deep Vietnamese crimson — `#C23528`; hover `#A82E22`
* **Neon signature (logo/brand mark only):** `#52E074`
* **Accent (for CTA buttons):** Anchor gold used at full saturation — no separate accent color needed

### Usage ratio

* Neutrals (base + surface + text): 65%
* Gold anchor: 20%
* Crimson secondary: 10%
* Neon signature: logo only — not counted in page ratio

### State color rule

* Default state colors acceptable: No
* Explanation: Default blue links and grey buttons will visually break the warm dark system. All interactive states must use gold or cream on dark backgrounds.

### Color anti-patterns

Avoid:
* Pure white backgrounds — destroys the evening/bar atmosphere entirely
* Bright red as the primary brand color — signals Chinese restaurant, not Vietnamese street bar
* Teal or cool-tone palettes — wrong temperature for this brand
* Gradient overlays (tech-style) on food photography — weakens the authenticity of the interior shots
* Decorative gold shimmer textures — looks cheap on a dark base; keep gold flat and clean
* Using neon green outside the logo mark — confirmed logo-only

## 5. Typography direction

### Typeface strategy

Hybrid — sans primary with serif display accent

### Typography role split

* **Primary reading font:** **Be Vietnam Pro** — body, UI, navigation, labels (~90%)
* **Accent / display font:** **Cormorant Garamond** — hero taglines, section pull quotes, special display moments (~10%)
* **Approx usage ratio:** 90% / 10%

**Why Be Vietnam Pro:** The name is a direct cultural signal — Vietnamese identity encoded in the typeface. It is clean, modern, and highly readable at all sizes. It bridges the "modernized traditional" aesthetic without leaning either too corporate or too casual.

**Why Cormorant Garamond:** High-contrast letterforms with a slightly classical weight. At large display sizes on a dark background in gold, it reads as warm and heritage-adjacent without being antiquated or generic. The contrast ratio between its thick and thin strokes gives it character that all-sans systems lack.

### Tone

Editorial / Human with a warm contemporary edge

### Density

Balanced — generous line-height on dark backgrounds for readability

### Hierarchy

Strong — dark backgrounds flatten visual hierarchy quickly; strong type weight contrasts are essential to keep the page scannable

### Heading behavior

* Sentence case
* Tight tracking (slightly negative on display sizes)
* Bold — Cormorant Garamond Italic at large scale for hero; Be Vietnam Pro Bold for section headings

### Body behavior

* Plain / clear
* Reading tone: warm, direct, present tense — matches voice cues
* Default weight: Regular (400) for body; Medium (500) for labels and nav

### Signature move

Hero tagline set in Cormorant Garamond Italic at display scale (64px+), warm gold `#F2B928`, on the dark near-black base — appears once at the top of the page and once as a section separator.

### Typography anti-patterns

Avoid:
* Serif body text everywhere — fine dining signal, wrong for a casual bar crowd
* Generic SaaS font pairings (Inter + DM Sans) — visually indistinguishable from every startup template
* All-caps tracking abuse on body copy — kills readability and adds unnecessary formality
* Decorative Vietnamese calligraphy fonts — culturally reductive and hard to read at small sizes
* Low-contrast text (light gray on dark) — accessibility failure on a dark base
* Multiple display fonts — keep Cormorant Garamond as the only personality font; everything else is Be Vietnam Pro

## 6. Layout rhythm

### Layout mode

Flow-based — immersive sections that build an evening narrative, not a modular app-style grid

### Grid

Flexible — allow full-bleed sections to coexist with contained content columns

### Rhythm

Alternating — dark full-bleed sections alternate with slightly lighter card/content sections to create visual breathing room

### Density

Balanced — not cramped; the photography is the primary communication asset and needs space

### Section pacing

Narrative — the page should read as an invitation to a night out, not a feature list

### Hero structure

**Editorial stack** — full-bleed dark interior image (bar/lantern atmosphere), large Cormorant Garamond Italic tagline, gold "Order Now" primary CTA, gold-outline "Book a Party" secondary CTA. Both CTAs are visible above the fold. Text overlaid on the image with a dark gradient scrim.

### Common section patterns

1. Full-width narrative (hero, atmosphere sections)
2. Card grid (menu items: 3-column on desktop)
3. Proof band (review strip with stars + quote)
4. CTA band (Book a Party — full width, gold button)
5. Quote section (highlighted 5-star review with large quote mark)

### Mobile behavior

* **What must remain true on mobile:** The hero atmosphere image, both CTAs above the fold, and the review proof signal
* **What may simplify on mobile:** Card grid collapses to single column; CTA band stacks vertically; navigation condenses to hamburger

### Layout anti-patterns

Avoid:
* Dashboard/app-style card grid with filter controls — wrong for a restaurant, signals SaaS
* Cramped sections with no breathing room around the photography
* Long unbroken copy walls — voice is short sentences; layout must match
* Multi-column text blocks for the hero — keep the hero hero, with one strong headline
* Symmetrical two-column feature lists — too corporate for this brand's energy

## 7. Component language

### Overall feel

Soft / Rounded — approachable and warm, not sharp and corporate

### Card language

Flat with a subtle warm-border (`#5C4232` at low opacity) — no drop shadows on a dark background; border defines the card, shadow doesn't

### Button language

* **Primary:** Filled, Rounded rectangle, Assertive — gold `#F2B928` on dark background, Be Vietnam Pro Bold, uppercase for primary CTA only
* **Secondary:** Outline, Rounded rectangle, Quiet — gold border `#F2B928`, gold text for "Book a Party" (distinct from tertiary cream)
* **Tertiary:** Ghost / text-link — cream underline for nav-level actions

### Form language

Warm / Minimal — dark input backgrounds with gold focus ring; no clinical white form fields

### Navigation feel

Direct + Premium — dark sticky header, Be Vietnam Pro Medium, cream text, gold accent on active state; no mega-menus

### Proof modules

1. Review strip (Google stars + short quote on gold band)
2. Quote block (featured 5-star review at large scale with Cormorant Garamond serif quote mark)
3. Trust banner (business history: "formerly Long's Noodle House — same family, same address")
4. Credential bar (Yelp 4.0★, Google rating, karaoke night promo)
5. Testimonial cards (2–3 reviews in a horizontal scroll on mobile)

### Signature component

**Bar promo strip** — a full-width horizontal section set in deep crimson `#C23528` background with gold text announcing: "1 free spring roll with any bar drink. Sit at the bar." Single line, Be Vietnam Pro Bold, gold CTA "See the menu." Appears above the footer. This is the only red-dominant section on the page.

### Component anti-patterns

Avoid:
* App-style tab bars or filter pills — wrong category
* Overdesigned menu cards with hover flip animations
* Icon-heavy service rows — use photography instead
* Multiple button colors on the same section — primary gold filled, secondary gold outline, no more variants
* Generic star-icon rating widgets styled like Google/Yelp knockoffs

Place (bar, interior atmosphere) first; Product (food close-ups) second; People (group energy at the bar) third — full photo library confirmed available

## 9. Motion and interaction

### Motion level

Restrained — the dark atmospheric base does not need animation to create mood; photography and color carry the weight

### Motion role

Reveal + Hover — content fades in as user scrolls; interactive elements respond to hover with minimal tactile feedback

### Button interaction

Tactile — primary gold button dims slightly on hover (gold `#F2B928` → `#E0A820`), with a 150ms ease transition. No scale transform, no glow effect.

### Scroll behavior

Fade-up — sections fade in from 12px below with 300ms ease as they enter the viewport. Not staggered, not dramatic.

### Performance rule

* **What should animate:** Section entry (fade-up), button hover state, image lazy-load fade-in
* **What should never animate:** The navigation header, the proof band, the CTA button text

### Reduced motion rule

Required: Yes — all scroll animations must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`

### Motion anti-patterns

Avoid:
* Parallax scrolling on the hero image — it fights the dark atmospheric base
* Floating menu or sticky order widget that follows scroll
* Animated number counters — trust-weakening gimmick
* Page transitions or route animations — not a SPA
* Hover sparkle or glow on food photography

## 10. Do / Don't rules

### Do

* Use the existing dark moody interior photography as the visual anchor for every atmospheric section
* Name the cocktail and dishes specifically in visual callouts — "Spicy Pho Ti-Ni," not "signature cocktail"
* Put the gold CTA button above the fold on every major section
* Use Be Vietnam Pro Bold for all short-sentence copy — the voice cues are punchy and the typeface delivers that
* Keep the bar promo strip (free spring roll) visible early in the page flow — it converts bar visitors
* Signal Saturday karaoke night with its own section or callout — it is a unique differentiator
* Use warm gold `#F2B928` for all interactive emphasis — one accent color, consistently applied
* Allow the photography to breathe — generous padding around food shots on the dark base
* Use the "formerly Long's Noodle House" lineage as trust copy — business history is proof on a new brand
* Show both CTAs (Order Now + Book a Party) above the fold in the hero

### Don't

* Don't use red as the dominant color on any page — it belongs to accent and the bar promo strip only
* Don't add any delivery copy until the client confirms the platform and delivery zone
* Don't use stock photography of any kind — the existing photo library is the asset
* Don't use a light or white background on any section except optional contrast cards
* Don't introduce a third typeface — Be Vietnam Pro + Cormorant Garamond is the complete system
* Don't use emojis, fire icons, or "🔥 hot items" callouts in the UI — undermines cultural credibility
* Don't add a language switcher unless confirmed need exists
* Don't use "authentic" as a standalone descriptor — replace with the specific dish or tradition every time
* Don't show the logo neon green (`#52E074`) anywhere except the logo mark itself
* Don't apply parallax to the hero image
