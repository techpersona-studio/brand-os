01 — Visual north star

What this brand must feel like

The brand should feel like a Vietnamese street corner bar at night — warm, atmospheric, and worth leaving the house for. Familiar enough to be welcoming, specific enough to be remembered.

Not like a family pho delivery chain, a generic pan-Asian restaurant, or a corporate dining experience dressed in Vietnamese colors.

One-line visual thesis

Street-born Vietnamese energy — warm, moody, and built for a night out.

02 — Direction mix

Editorial / Human leads

Editorial / Human — 70% Cutting-edge / Innovative — 30%

Editorial / Human (70%) provides the warm, atmosphere-first visual language that moves an 18–35 crowd from browsing to "going out tonight" — it sells the feeling of being there before it sells the menu. The Cutting-edge / Innovative layer (30%) adds nightlife credibility so the bar reads as a real destination, not an afterthought. Equal balance between the two would produce a cold, trend-forward aesthetic that strips the cultural warmth this brand needs to stand apart.

What this mix prevents

  • Bright white restaurant template with generic pho bowl photography — interchangeable with every suburban Asian restaurant
  • SaaS-style card grid with filter tabs and metric chips — wrong category, wrong trust signal entirely
  • Fine-dining editorial overhead shots on white — wrong audience, wrong price signal, wrong mood
  • Generic "Asian fusion neon bar" aesthetic with decorative neon as wallpaper — this brand's neon is specific (the QUÁN PHỞ sign)
03 — Color system

Dark warmth. One gold. One crimson.

Five dark tones from near-black to warm brown build an evening atmosphere — with warm cream text throughout. Golden-yellow (--anchor) drives all interactivity: CTAs, active states, and callouts. Deep crimson (--accent) appears in exactly one location: the bar promo strip.

Usage ratio

Neutral dark tones carry 65% of every page — they set the evening atmosphere. Gold anchor carries 20% — every button, link, and callout. Crimson is intentionally rare at 10%, confined to the bar promo strip alone.

Never do this

  • Pure white backgrounds on any section — destroys the evening atmosphere the brand is built on
  • Crimson as a dominant or section background color — bar promo strip only, always controlled
  • Cool-toned grays or blues — wrong temperature; this palette runs entirely warm
  • Gradient overlays (tech-style) on food photography — weakens the authenticity of the interior shots
  • Neon green #52E074 outside the logo mark — it signals "Asian fusion neon bar" if used as a UI color
04 — Typography system

Be Vietnam Pro leads. Cormorant Garamond accents.

Be Vietnam Pro carries 90% of all typographic work — body, navigation, labels, and the short punchy copy that defines this brand's voice. The name itself is a direct cultural signal. Cormorant Garamond Italic appears at display scale (64px+) in gold for the hero tagline and one pull quote only — it creates a single moment of heritage warmth per page without competing with the photography.

05 — Layout rhythm

Flow-based narrative scroll

Each section builds an evening invitation — from atmosphere (hero) through the experience (bar, karaoke), to proof (reviews), and into action (order, book). It must never read as a feature list, a delivery menu, or a card-grid catalog.

Section banding sequence

Navigation --base · sticky header
Hero --base · full-bleed photo overlay
Bar & experience --core · bar, cocktails intro
Menu highlights --base · 3-column food card grid
Bar promo strip --accent · the only crimson section
Karaoke night --core · Saturday 8PM callout
Proof --base-deep · review strip + trust banner
Book a party --anchor-dark · primary conversion CTA

No two adjacent sections share the same background token — alternate between base, core, and base-deep to maintain visual rhythm without breaking atmosphere.

07 — Proof presentation

Real proof. Earned signals only.

Quán Phở launched in 2025 with 9 Yelp reviews and a medium trust burden. The strategy is to lead with what's undeniably real — the physical bar, professional food photography, family business history, and specific named experiences. Trust volume grows naturally. Nothing is manufactured.

Phase 1 — launch (now)

Proof available immediately

  • Bar promo strip — confirmed: "1 free spring roll with any bar drink. Sit at the bar."
  • Saturday karaoke — confirmed: every week at 8PM (specific, verifiable, differentiated)
  • 13 professional food photos + 3 interior shots — high-quality existing assets
  • "Formerly Long's Noodle House — same family, same address" — business history as trust copy
  • 3 × 5-star Google reviews — quote the best one verbatim on the homepage
  • Spicy Pho Ti-Ni signature cocktail — name it specifically; generic "cocktails" loses the proof value
Phase 2 — first reviews arrive

As social proof grows

  • Activate review strip — Yelp and Google star ratings displayed as a gold band with a real quoted review
  • Replace homepage placeholder with a specific 5-star quote: real name, real experience, real dish mentioned
  • Add credential bar with live Yelp star count once it exceeds 4.0 with meaningful volume (20+ reviews)
  • Group/party photos as they accumulate — real crowd energy, real bar shots, never stock

What must never be fabricated

  • Review counts or star ratings not yet earned — never round up or estimate Yelp/Google scores
  • Party booking flow, capacity numbers, or deposit amounts before confirmed by client
  • Delivery capability or delivery zone before the platform and area are confirmed
  • Group energy shots using stock photography — use only real bar/crowd photos from the existing library
  • Any pricing, reservation, or operational details that haven't been provided directly by the client

09 — Motion and interaction

Restrained. Purposeful. Never decorative.

Motion rules

LevelRestrained — photography and color carry the atmospheric weight; motion adds nothing extra
RoleReveal + Hover — scroll entry fades, interactive states respond
Scroll behaviorFade-up: sections enter from 12px below at 300ms ease-soft. Not staggered. Not dramatic.
Button interactionTactile — gold dims on hover (#F2B928 → #E0A820) at 150ms ease. No scale transform. No glow effect.
Propertiesopacity and transform only — no layout-triggering properties
Reduced motionRequired — all animations must check prefers-reduced-motion and disable gracefully

What must never animate

  • The hero image — no parallax; it fights the dark atmospheric base and kills performance
  • The navigation header — sticky nav must be completely static on scroll
  • Background color transitions on any section band
  • Proof numbers or review counts — animated counters are a trust-weakening gimmick
  • Hover sparkle, glow, or shimmer effects on food photography
  • Floating persistent widgets that follow the user's scroll position

Easing curves

--ease: cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1) — UI transitions

--ease-soft: cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1) — scroll reveals

11 — Do / Don't rules

Blunt. Visual. Operational.

Do

  • Use existing dark moody interior photography as the visual anchor for every atmospheric section
  • Name the cocktail and dishes specifically in callouts — "Spicy Pho Ti-Ni," not "signature cocktail"
  • Put the gold CTA button above the fold on every major section
  • Show both CTAs (Order Now + Book a Party) in the hero — both are live
  • Use Be Vietnam Pro Bold for all short-sentence copy
  • Keep the bar promo strip (free spring roll) visible early in the page flow
  • Signal Saturday karaoke night with its own dedicated section or callout
  • Use warm gold #F2B928 for all interactive emphasis — one accent color, consistently applied
  • Allow photography to breathe — generous padding on the dark base
  • Use "formerly Long's Noodle House" lineage as trust copy

Don't

  • Don't use red as the dominant color on any page — crimson belongs to accent and bar promo strip only
  • Don't add delivery copy until confirmed by client
  • Don't use any stock photography — the existing photo library is the asset
  • Don't use a light or white background on any full-page section
  • Don't introduce a third typeface — Be Vietnam Pro + Cormorant Garamond is the complete system
  • Don't use emojis, fire icons, or "hot items" callouts in the UI
  • Don't use the neon green #52E074 outside the logo mark
  • Don't use "authentic" as a standalone descriptor — replace with the specific dish or tradition
  • Don't apply parallax effects to the hero image
  • Don't add a language switcher without confirmed bilingual content need

13 — Implementation notes

What must be preserved. What can flex.

Must preserve exactly

  • All colors must reference token variables — never hardcode hex values outside the :root block
  • Double-shell card structure: outer rgba(255,255,255,.04) shell + inner var(--core) — do not collapse to a single layer
  • Section background sequence (base → core → base-deep → anchor-dark) — alternating bands define the page rhythm
  • Cormorant Garamond Italic confined to display/hero scale only — never appears in body copy, labels, or navigation
  • Sentence case on all headings — never title case, never all-caps headings
  • Reduced-motion guard on every CSS animation and scroll reveal — @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)
  • Crimson (--accent) used for the bar promo strip only — not for buttons, links, or any other section background

Accessibility

  • Focus-visible ring uses var(--anchor) at 2px offset — visible on both dark and warm-brown backgrounds
  • Color is never the sole information carrier — always pair with shape, label, or icon
  • All food and interior photos require descriptive alt text (e.g. "red lanterns and wooden tables inside Quán Phở")
  • Form labels must be visible — no placeholder-only labeling on any input field
  • Minimum tap target 44×44px on all interactive elements including nav links on mobile
  • Primary text (--ink #F0E8D0) on base (#181008) achieves ~13:1 contrast ratio — passes WCAG AA and AAA
  • All scroll reveals and animations must disable under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce

Future agents must never improvise

  • No second accent color — gold and crimson are the complete brand color set; no third introduction
  • No gradient overlays or glassmorphism effects on any section or card
  • No light or white section backgrounds — this is a dark-base system by design, not by accident
  • No Cormorant Garamond in body copy, UI labels, or navigation — display/hero only, max two uses per page
  • No stock photography of any kind — the existing photo library is the only asset approved for use
