# Visual Strategist Decision Brief

## Purpose

This document is the final human-reviewed decision brief between Business Persona and Branding Visual Guide generation.

Its job is to:
- convert business and conversion context into visual decisions
- resolve ambiguity through human-in-the-loop questions
- lock enough direction that the final Branding Visual Guide can be generated consistently

This is not the final client guide.
This is the decision input for generating the final guide.

## 2. Visual direction recommendation

### Primary direction

Editorial / Human

### Secondary influence

Enterprise / Stable

### Direction mix

- Primary %: 65%
- Secondary %: 35%

### Why this direction

- The brand IS Thao — the entire trust model depends on a named person, not a studio. Editorial/Human is the only direction that can carry first-person singular voice, "one person on your project," and a solo Craftsperson archetype.
- Audience are non-tech owners (plumbers, restaurant owners). Cutting-edge/innovative would create distance; editorial/human reads accessible and trustworthy to them.
- Buyer trigger is emotional (invisible cost of missed leads). Human-warm framing converts better than features-and-specs layouts.
- High trust burden for a new operator demands warmth and directness, not authority or polish. Enterprise/Stable provides the credibility underpinning (pine anchor, structured proof modules) without tipping into agency or SaaS territory.
- The free audit CTA must feel low-pressure and approachable — editorial/human supports this; cutting-edge does not.

### Must avoid

- Blue/gray SaaS palette with gradient hero — the generic web design category color; kills the differentiation this brand depends on
- Agency multi-service grid with team headshots and partner badge rows — wrong positioning entirely; this is a solo craftsperson, not a firm
- Stock laptop/handshake photography over hero backgrounds — signals template-built, not hand-built, to the exact audience this brand is trying to earn
- Workflow node diagrams or dashboard UI screenshots — technology must stay "behind the wall"; showing it creates distance for non-tech business owners
- Heavy testimonial carousels with no real name or photo attribution — fabricated-looking proof is fatal for a new solo operator whose main objection is trust
- Glassmorphism or frosted-blur content areas — generic SaaS aesthetic this brand explicitly rejects
- Purple/blue AI-vendor color schemes — audience associates these with "tech overwhelm," the exact problem being solved
- Oversized icon-and-benefit card grids — product landing page feel; audience needs human-first, not feature-first

## 4. Color direction

### Primary color logic

- Recommended anchor color family: Deep pine (#1E3A34) — matches existing confirmed brand
- Why: Pine differentiates from the blue/gray SaaS and web-agency category default. It reads as stable, trustworthy, and grounded without being trendy or tech-forward. The warmth of pine over cold navy or teal is the distinction.
- Domain signal: Non-standard for web design category (most competitors run blue/gray) — this is a differentiator, not a liability
- Emotional role: Trust anchor, gravitas, permanence — "the fixed thing in a fast-talking industry"

### Base color logic

- Base: Light
- Neutral temperature: Warm (stone-paper, never pure white or cool gray)
- Contrast level: Medium-high (warm stone body + high-contrast dark pine sections)

### Accent logic

- Accent usage: Rare (~8% of any view)
- Accent role: Single primary CTA per page + italic Fraunces emphasis moments
- Accent caution: Never cover large areas; never compete with pine; never use for secondary actions, decorative elements, or section backgrounds

### Palette recommendation

- Neutral direction: Warm stone-paper (#F6F3EE default, #EFEAE1 alternate band, #FBFAF7 card interior) — never pure #FFF
- Anchor direction: Deep pine (#1E3A34), pine-soft (#2E5148 hover), pine-wash (#E8EFEC tinted band)
- Secondary direction: None
- Accent direction: Terracotta (#C06A45), terra-deep (#9F4F33 hover), terra-light (#E7A282 on dark pine)

### Usage ratio

- Neutrals: ~60%
- Primary (pine + ink): ~30%
- Accent (terracotta): ~8–10%
- Secondary: 0%

### State color rule

- Default state colors acceptable: No
- Explanation: Default browser-blue focus rings and bright-red error states introduce colors outside the palette. Focus rings should use pine or terracotta. Error states should use a warm desaturated red.

### Color anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Pure #FFF backgrounds (cold, clinical, generic)
- Tech blue/purple gradient heroes (SaaS associations)
- Adding any second accent beyond terracotta (teal, gold, navy)
- Large terracotta fields (it's a spice, not a base)
- Using pine-wash as a primary page background (band color only)

## 5. Typography direction

### Typeface strategy

Hybrid (sans primary, serif accent)

### Typography role split

- Primary reading font: Hanken Grotesk — body, UI, labels, and most headings (~90%)
- Accent / display font: Fraunces italic — hero headline moments and one emphasis phrase per section (~10%)
- Approx usage ratio: 90/10

### Tone

Human (Hanken keeps it operationally readable; Fraunces italic adds editorial soul without pulling the brand into newspaper territory)

### Density

Spacious (generous whitespace; the brand targets busy owners who skim on mobile)

### Hierarchy

Strong (clear visual distance between hero statement, section heading, body copy, and label/kicker)

### Heading behavior

- Sentence case always (never Title Case)
- Tight tracking: `letter-spacing: -.045em` at display sizes
- Confident weight: 700–800 Hanken for primary headings

### Body behavior

- Plain / operational
- Reading tone: Direct, short, one idea per sentence
- Default weight: 400

### Signature move

Italic Fraunces phrase in terracotta (#C06A45), once per section, at display size. Fragment or proper noun only — never a full sentence. This is the editorial soul of the brand; overuse destroys it.

### Typography anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Serif body text anywhere
- Inter, Roboto, or system font stacks (generic)
- Title Case headings
- Multiple display fonts alongside Fraunces
- Fraunces for body or UI text
- Tracked all-caps at heading scale (kickers only — small, label-level)

## 6. Layout rhythm

### Layout mode

Flow-based (narrative scroll; not modular dashboard or card grid)

### Grid

Standard (12-column, max-width ~1200px, consistent gutters)

### Rhythm

Alternating (stone / deep stone / pine-wash / dark pine — no two adjacent section bands share a shade)

### Density

Spacious (generous section padding; skimmable for mobile business owners)

### Section pacing

Calm / Narrative (problem → invisible consequence → solution → proof → CTA — story flow, not feature dump)

### Hero structure

Text + portrait (founder photo is confirmed and is the primary trust instrument; no product screenshot, dashboard, or abstract graphic)

### Common section patterns

1. Full-width narrative (problem/pain section — copy-led, no imagery distraction)
2. 2-column (service split: Website / Quiet Systems side by side)
3. Proof band (floating proof chips + testimonial structure, once reviews exist)
4. Process section (Step 1 / 2 / 3 — already confirmed on current site)
5. CTA band (terracotta accent CTA, dark pine background)

### Mobile behavior

- What must remain true on mobile: Founder portrait in hero, single-column reading flow, CTA visible above the fold
- What may simplify on mobile: 2-column sections stack; process steps become vertical list; proof chips reduce to 1–2

### Layout anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Dashboard-feel or card-metric grids
- Side-by-side feature comparison tables
- Sticky floating chat or bot widgets
- Multiple competing CTAs in the same section
- Long unbroken copy walls

## 7. Component language

### Overall feel

Soft / Rounded (squircle corners throughout; warm, never brutalist)

### Card language

Double-shell: pine-tinted outer shell (`rgba(30,58,52,.045)`, hairline border, ~2rem radius, ~10px padding) wrapping an inner core (`#FBFAF7`, concentric radius, faint inset highlight). Cards feel machined, not flat.

### Button language

- Filled pill (primary pine) / Terracotta fill for single most important CTA per page
- Island button: nested circular icon wrapper holding `→`, magnetic hover with icon drift up-right
- Ghost: text with 2px ink underline, underline goes terracotta on hover — secondary actions only

### Form language

High-trust: minimal fields, no overwhelm. Audit form: name + website URL + email only. No multi-step wizards.

### Navigation feel

Quiet / Direct (no mega-menu; flat nav with single CTA button in the top right)

### Proof modules

1. Founder note (first-person text block with portrait — essential for solo brand trust)
2. Floating proof chips (outcome illustrations: "New booking · 9:48 pm", "Replied in 2 min" — clearly illustrative until real)
3. Credential bar (6 years software engineering — specific and honest, not a badge farm)
4. Testimonial cards (placeholder structure now; fill with real names + photos once available)

### Signature component

Island button — a filled pill with a nested circular icon wrapper holding `→`, soft magnetic hover with diagonal icon drift. Pine fill throughout; terracotta variant reserved for the single audit CTA per page.

### Component anti-patterns

Avoid:
- App-style sidebar navigation or dashboard chrome
- Overdesigned service cards stacking icons + badges + pills simultaneously
- Multiple button variants competing in the same section
- Fake SaaS widgets or bot UI preview components
- Award/badge clusters without earned and verifiable credentials

## 8. Imagery and art direction

### Photography direction

Founder-led (Thao's portrait is the primary trust signal; founder photo confirmed present on site)

### Subject priority

People (Thao) first, Proof (outcome moments) second

### Mood

Warm / Intimate

### Lighting

Natural / Soft (not studio-cold; not moody or high-contrast)

### Composition

- Close to Medium shots
- Polished but candid (not posed corporate headshot)

### Proof imagery

- What proof should appear visually: Floating notification cards ("New inquiry · 9:48 pm"), before/after site comparison, step-completion moments
- What should never be fabricated: Review counts, star ratings, revenue numbers, named clients without explicit permission

### Illustration / icon direction

Minimal outline icons only — service section and process steps; never decorative

### Imagery anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Generic stock photography (laptops, handshakes, "team at work")
- Phone chatbot UI or automation workflow diagrams
- Dashboard screenshots
- Before/after comparisons without real attribution
- Cold overlit studio portraits that read corporate
- Cliché category visuals (magnifying glass on laptop, gears on a screen)

## 9. Motion and interaction

### Motion level

Restrained

### Motion role

Reveal (scroll-triggered) + Hover (button interaction)

### Button interaction

Magnetic — the island button drifts a few pixels toward the cursor; the nested icon translates diagonally on hover

### Scroll behavior

Fade-up — elements fade and rise once as they enter the viewport; no repeat on re-scroll

### Performance rule

- What should animate: `transform` and `opacity` only (entrance reveals, button hover)
- What should never animate: background color transitions, layout shifts, imagery on scroll (no parallax), text content

### Reduced motion rule

Required — every animation guarded by `prefers-reduced-motion`

### Motion anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Decorative looping or ambient animations
- Parallax backgrounds
- Floating widgets or persistent animated elements
- Transitions that delay reading content
- Animations firing on every scroll event (only once on enter)

## 10. Do / don't rules

### Do

1. Lead every section with the problem or the cost of inaction — solution second
2. Keep Thao's first-person voice in all copy blocks ("I build," "I'll send back," "you reach me directly") — never "we"
3. Keep the founder portrait visible in the hero above the fold on all viewports
4. Use pine as the trust anchor throughout; never dilute with additional brand colors
5. Use floating proof chips to illustrate outcomes before real reviews exist — label them as illustrative if needed
6. Limit terracotta to a single primary CTA per page
7. Alternate section backgrounds (stone / deep stone / pine-wash / dark pine) so no two adjacent bands match
8. Use sentence case for all headings — no exceptions
9. Place the free audit CTA in the hero and the final closing section at minimum

### Don't

1. Use stock photography of laptops, code, handshakes, or generic "team at work" scenes
2. Add a second accent color for any reason
3. Show workflow diagrams, node maps, automation flows, or dashboard screenshots anywhere
4. Write in plural voice ("our team," "we build," "our clients") anywhere on the site
5. Display testimonials, reviews, or star ratings without a real name and real photo
6. Use pure #FFF backgrounds — warm stone (#F6F3EE) only
7. Use Fraunces for body or UI text — italic accent at display size only
8. Fabricate metrics, star counts, client revenue numbers, or "AI transformation" results
9. Place more than one CTA in a single section
10. Use Title Case for any heading
