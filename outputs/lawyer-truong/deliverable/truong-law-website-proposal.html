What I found

Twelve things costing you clients.

Getting found

4
  • Someone searching for a criminal lawyer in Boston will not find you. Your page is titled "Lawyertruong" instead of what you do.
  • Someone searching in Vietnamese will not find you either. Both languages sit on one web address, so Google only ever sees one of them.
  • Where you do come up, you rank lower than you should. Old copies of pages are still live, competing with the real ones.
  • Your twenty-two years of standing is split in half. The site answers on two addresses and Google treats them as two sites.

First impression

3
  • You read as smaller and newer than you are. The colours do not hold together across the page.
  • People lose attention before they reach what they came for. The homepage is a wall of text.
  • Nobody is asked to get in touch. The only buttons on your pages switch language. Nothing else invites a call.

Turning visitors into clients

5
  • Someone convinced by your record has nowhere to go. The homepage reads like a biography, not a page built to get a call.
  • A new client has no reason to trust you yet. You have 4.4 stars from 29 Google reviews, and not one of them appears on your site.
  • Your practice pages explain the law, not why to call you. The auto accident page describes tort law and contingency fees. It never says Boston, never says Vietnamese, and never mentions you.
  • Your best story is a picture nobody can read. The Boston Globe piece on your journey from Vietnam is a scan of a newspaper page. Unreadable on a phone, invisible to Google.
  • People give up before finishing your form. It sits on another page and asks for six things, including a home address.

Speed, phone display and where your form actually goes need a look at the code. That is the first thing I do.
