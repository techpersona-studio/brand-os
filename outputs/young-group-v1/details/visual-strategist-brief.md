# Visual Strategist Decision Brief

## Purpose

This document is the final human-reviewed decision brief between Business Persona and Branding Visual Guide generation.

Its job is to convert business and conversion context into visual decisions, resolve ambiguity through human-in-the-loop questions, and lock enough direction for consistent final guide generation.

This is not the final client guide. It is the decision input for generating the final guide.

---

## 1. Input summary from Business Persona

### Business

- **Brand name:** Young Group (entity) / Trần Thủy Tiên (founder and brand face)
- **Category:** Weight loss coaching / health and wellness — science-based, personalized, long-term
- **Offer:** 1-on-1 coaching until goal reached; includes weekly meal plan, 42-meal food combo shipped, daily tracking, weekly Zoom education sessions, lifetime maintenance formula, money-back guarantee
- **Service area:** Global — active in USA, Vietnam, Australia, Canada
- **Primary CTA:** TƯ VẤN CHO TÔI (Book free consultation call)
- **Sales model:** Consultation-first; price revealed post-call; 50% discount with countdown timer on homepage
- **Trust burden:** High — health category, private pricing by design, formal credentials unconfirmed, no third-party ratings
- **Buyer urgency:** Medium — chronic pain point, urgency manufactured at decision moment via discount timer

### Audience

- **Primary audience:** Vietnamese-speaking women, ages 30–55, across USA / Vietnam / Australia / Canada — long-term weight struggle, tried other methods without lasting results
- **Secondary audience:** Open to all genders; Vietnamese-speaking men at lower volume
- **Buyer trigger:** Persistent failure with prior programs; desire for permanent, root-cause fix
- **Main objection:** "I've tried everything. Why would this be different?"
- **Proof needed:** Named testimonials with specific results (pounds, time, location); the "until goal reached" guarantee stated plainly; founder credibility (book, methodology, personal story)

### Brand

- **Desired feeling:** Understood. Empowered. In control.
- **Core traits:** Empathetic and warm; science-backed and methodical; aspirational; direct and confident; premium
- **Anti-traits:** Clinical or cold; generic diet-program tone; hard-sell or fear-based; supplement-dependent; preachy about willpower
- **Voice cues:** Address reader directly ("bạn"); lead with root cause, not shame; challenge limiting beliefs gently; use specific numbers in testimonials
- **Messaging cues:** "Bạn không có lỗi. Phương pháp có lỗi."; "No supplements. No exercise. No extreme diets. No regain."; "The program runs until you reach your goal. Guaranteed."

### Strategic notes

- **What the website must optimize for:** Trust-building before the consultation call. The buyer must feel understood, seen, and safe enough to book — the price conversion happens on the call, not the page.
- **What the brand must NOT feel like:** A generic diet app. A medical clinic. A gym transformation program. A supplement shop. Anything that uses shame or fear as motivation.
- **Unresolved business questions affecting visuals:** Formal credentials still unconfirmed — the visual system must be designed to accommodate a credential strip if confirmed, but must not depend on one.

## 2. Visual direction recommendation

### Primary direction

**Editorial / Human**

### Secondary influence

**Enterprise / Stable**

### Direction mix

- Primary (Editorial / Human): 65%
- Secondary (Enterprise / Stable): 35%

### Why this direction

- **Trust burden is high:** Buyers are skeptical and have failed before. Editorial / Human signals personal authenticity — a real person, a real story, a real method. Enterprise / Stable signals that the business is serious and will deliver.
- **Founder-led brand:** The face, voice, and personal story of Trần Thủy Tiên are the primary trust asset. Editorial / Human keeps her at the center without subordinating her to a system or brand architecture.
- **Audience psychology:** Vietnamese women aged 30–55 in diaspora contexts respond to warmth, respectful authority, and relatability — not tech-forward innovation or cold clinical precision.
- **Offer type:** An ongoing 1-on-1 coaching relationship is inherently human and intimate. The visual system must match the product feel.
- **Price positioning:** Premium service with private pricing requires a premium visual language — but warmth, not distance. Enterprise / Stable adds the trust scaffolding (methodology, proof, structure) that keeps the premium from feeling frivolous.
- **Emotional goal:** "Understood. Empowered. In control." — these are human, relational states. A cold or tech-forward visual system directly contradicts the desired feeling.

### Must avoid

- Generic weight-loss or diet brand aesthetics (before/after gym culture, calorie-counter UX)
- Clinical medical UI (cold whites, sterile layouts, health-app chrome)
- SaaS or app-style product design (feature grids, product screenshots, workflow diagrams)
- Supplement brand visual language (bright color bursts, energetic imagery, bold claims)
- Faceless wellness brand with stock-photo smiles and no personal story
- Hard-sell landing page conventions (excessive countdown timers, banner stacking, pressure tactics)
- Luxury fashion that loses warmth and becomes cold or intimidating

- **Not like:** A diet program website, a medical clinic, a supplement storefront, or a SaaS dashboard.

2. Trustworthy
3. Intimate

### 3 anti-adjectives

2. Transactional

Warm luxury wellness rooted in personal authority — premium, human, and never clinical.

## 4. Color direction

### Primary color logic

- **Recommended anchor color family:** Warm gold / champagne bronze
- **Why:** Confirmed by brand asset image — the logo, decorative elements, and overall palette use warm gold-champagne tones. Gold signals premium in the wellness and beauty category without clinical coldness. It also reads as aspirational and culturally resonant.
- **Domain signal:** Premium personal service, not a product brand or medical offering
- **Emotional role:** Aspiration, warmth, earned transformation — "you deserve this"

### Base color logic

- **Base:** Light
- **Neutral temperature:** Warm (cream/ivory, not cool white)
- **Contrast level:** Medium — the brand needs readable hierarchy but not high-contrast tech energy

### Accent logic

- **Accent usage:** Controlled
- **Accent role:** CTA primary + decorative emphasis. The gold anchor serves as the primary accent. A secondary soft warm tone (blush or warm taupe) may support imagery framing — tentative.
- **Accent caution:** Must never feel like a promotion banner or supplement shop. Gold accents must read as editorial marks, not discount signals.

### Palette recommendation

- **Neutral direction:** Warm ivory / cream — off-white with a slight warm undertone, not pure white. Background base for most sections.
- **Anchor direction:** Warm gold / champagne bronze — primary brand color. Used for logo, decorative marks, typographic accents, CTA fills or borders.
- **Secondary direction:** Deep warm charcoal / near-black — for text, headings, and structural elements. Not pure black — keep warmth.
- **Accent direction:** Soft blush / dusty rose — for proof section backgrounds, founder quote blocks, secondary card fills. Locked by feminine-luxury direction confirmation.

### Usage ratio

- Neutrals (cream / ivory): 60%
- Primary (gold / champagne): 20%
- Dark (charcoal / near-black): 15%
- Accent (blush / taupe): 5%

### State color rule

- **Default state colors acceptable:** No
- **If no:** Standard browser blue/red/green states break the warm premium palette. CTAs use gold or charcoal. Error states use warm deep amber, not red. Focus states use a warm gold ring.

### Color anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Pure white background — makes the brand feel clinical and cold
- Tech gradients — break the analog luxury feel
- Bright green or electric blue — category-wrong for wellness premium
- Multiple competing accent colors — dilutes the anchor color's authority
- Gold used decoratively without structural role — looks cheap, not premium
- Pure black text on pure white — kills warmth

## 5. Typography direction

### Typeface strategy

**Hybrid** — Bold display weight + script calligraphic accent + clean humanist sans for body

### Typography role split

- **Primary reading font:** Clean humanist sans — warm, contemporary, highly readable at all sizes
- **Accent / display font:** Script calligraphy — signature move, used 1–2 times per section maximum (confirmed from brand asset image)
- **Approx usage ratio:** 75% humanist sans, 25% script accent (display headings may use a bolder weight of the sans or a compatible display cut)

### Tone

**Human / Editorial hybrid** — warm and readable, with personal calligraphic signature as the emotional anchor

### Density

**Balanced** — generous spacing, not sparse/editorial minimalist and not cramped

### Hierarchy

**Strong** — bold display headline, clear body weight, script used for emotional emphasis only

### Heading behavior

- **Sentence case** (matches Vietnamese reading conventions; title case is culturally less natural)
- **Tight tracking** on display / bold headings
- **Expressive weight contrast** between headings and body — the headline should carry significant visual mass

### Body behavior

- **Editorial** — clean, generous line height, comfortable reading length (60–70 characters per line)
- **Reading tone:** Warm and direct, not technical
- **Default weight:** Regular / 400

### Signature move

**Script calligraphy phrase once per major section** — a flowing, hand-written-feel phrase that softens a bold headline. Preserved and refined: use more selectively than the current implementation, at higher quality (better typeface selection, more intentional placement), but not removed. This is the core typographic signature of the brand.

### Typography anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Serif body everywhere — too editorial, loses warmth at paragraph length
- Generic SaaS font pairing (Inter + Inter variations) — breaks premium feel
- All-caps body text — reads aggressive, not warm
- Too many type weights in a single section — two weights maximum per section
- Novelty or decorative display fonts beyond the script accent
- Low-legibility script at small sizes — script must only appear at display / subheadline scale

## 6. Layout rhythm

### Layout mode

**Flow-based** with modular proof sections

### Grid

**Flexible** — wide-margin centered layout with generous breathing room; no tight-grid dashboard structure

### Rhythm

**Alternating** — sections alternate between founder/narrative content and proof content, maintaining momentum without feeling repetitive

### Density

**Spacious** — generous vertical padding per section; the premium feel requires air

### Section pacing

**Narrative** — the page tells a story: problem → root cause → method → proof → founder → CTA

### Hero structure

**Text + portrait** — confirmed by brand asset image. Founder's photograph anchors the left or right; headline and subline anchor the other half. Must not feel like a stock-photo wellness site — the portrait must be of the actual founder.

### Common section patterns

- **Full-width narrative** — for the root-cause / method explanation
- **Proof band** — for testimonials with named results (inline band, not card grid)
- **Founder block** — dedicated founder story / authority section
- **CTA band** — single-focus, full-width consultation call section with urgency signal
- **2-column** — for side-by-side before/after or contrast (e.g., "What you've tried vs. what we do")
- **Process section** — for the four "không" pillars and the program steps
- **Quote section** — for key testimonials with named attribution and result specifics

### Mobile behavior

- **Must remain true on mobile:** The founder's face must remain visible above the fold. Script typography must remain readable (minimum 28px equivalent). Proof must not disappear or be buried.
- **May simplify on mobile:** The two-column alternating sections collapse to single column. Card grids compress to a single scrollable list.

### Layout anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Dashboard or grid-heavy structure — this is not an app
- Cluttered card stacks — maximum 3 testimonial cards visible at once
- Hyper-asymmetric layout without narrative purpose
- Long unbroken copy walls — break every 3–4 lines with visual breathing room
- Multiple competing CTAs on a single viewport — one primary CTA per section

## 7. Component language

### Overall feel

**Soft / Rounded** — warm, approachable, premium without being cold or sharp

### Card language

**Bordered** with soft warm shadow — a thin warm gold or cream border, slight elevation shadow in warm tone. No flat-zero cards — proof content needs gentle structure to feel trustworthy.

### Button language

- **Primary CTA:** Filled — warm gold or deep charcoal fill, sentence case label, rounded rectangle (not pill, not square)
- **Secondary:** Outline — thin gold or charcoal border, warm background fill on hover
- **Feel:** Premium — weight and spacing that communicates confidence, not urgency

### Form language

**High-trust** — clean field labels, generous padding, minimal decoration. The consultation booking form is a trust moment — it must never feel lightweight or generic.

### Navigation feel

**Premium / Quiet** — low visual noise, sticky on scroll, logo anchored left, CTA button anchored right. Transparent or cream background depending on page section.

### Proof modules

This brand should use:
- **Quote blocks** — full testimonial text with name, location, and specific result (e.g., "Giảm 12kg trong 60 ngày — Hà Nội")
- **Results chips** — small inline indicators of specific outcomes (name, location, pounds, timeframe)
- **Founder note** — a personal section where Trần Thủy Tiên speaks directly to the reader
- **Trust banner** — the four "không" pillars displayed as an icon strip
- **Before/after gallery** — only with approved client photos; never fabricated
- **Process band** — program steps explained visually (booking → plan → coaching → goal)

### Signature component

**Founder quote slab** — a full-bleed or near-full-bleed section where the founder's voice appears in large script or editorial type, set against the warm cream/ivory background, with her signature below. This is the brand's most trust-building component — it must feel intimate, not promotional.

### Component anti-patterns

Avoid:
- App-style UI chrome (tabs, toggles, pills that look like filter controls)
- Overdesigned cards with gradients, icons, and badges stacked together
- Decorative icons with no functional role
- Multiple button variants on the same page section — pick one
- Fake SaaS widgets (countdown timers styled as dashboards, pricing tables with feature matrices)

## 8. Imagery and art direction

### Photography direction

**Founder-led** — Trần Thủy Tiên is the primary visual asset. Supporting imagery: real client testimonials if approved, food combo product, lifestyle context (consultation, wellness environment).

### Subject priority

1. People (founder first, then clients if approved)
2. Product (42-meal food combo — authentic, styled warmly)
3. Proof (before/after only if approved and real)
4. Object detail (meal close-ups, book cover, warmth lifestyle props)

### Mood

**Warm / Intimate** — the brand must feel personal and trusted, not aspirational-distant

### Lighting

**Natural / Soft** — warm natural light, consistent with brand image evidence. No hard studio lighting, no high-contrast dramatic editorial lighting.

### Composition

- **Medium** framing for founder portraits (from shoulders up; full body for select shots)
- **Editorial / Polished Candid** — professional photography that reads as authentic, not staged. The founder should be photographed in genuine expressions, not stiff poses.

### Proof imagery

- **What proof should appear visually:** Named testimonials with real client photos if approved; specific numbers (kg/pounds, timeframe, location) must be visible as text alongside photos
- **What should never be fabricated:** Before/after images; client results; stock-photo testimonials. Only use real assets.

### Illustration / icon direction

**Outline** — thin, warm-toned outline icons, as confirmed by brand asset image. Used only for the four "không" pillars and program step indicators. No decorative illustration beyond this.

### Imagery anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Generic stock wellness smiles (woman running, salad bowl, measuring tape)
- Phone mockups or app screenshots
- Clinical photography (white lab coats, medical equipment)
- Gym culture imagery (weights, sweat, transformation montages)
- Over-filtered or heavily retouched photography that breaks authenticity
- Fake before/after results of any kind

## 9. Motion and interaction

### Motion level

**Restrained**

### Motion role

**Reveal / Hover** — content reveals on scroll, subtle hover states on interactive elements

### Button interaction

**Tactile** — a gentle scale or fill-shift on hover that confirms interactivity without showmanship

### Scroll behavior

**Fade-up** — sections and proof elements fade up gently as they enter the viewport. Not a dramatic sequence or parallax.

### Performance rule

- **What should animate:** Section entrance reveals (fade-up), button hover states, navigation transitions
- **What should never animate:** Proof content (testimonials must be instantly readable), founder photography, pricing or guarantee text

### Reduced motion rule

**Required: Yes** — the audience is 30–55, health-focused. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` fully. No fallback motion on reduced-motion preference.

### Motion anti-patterns

Avoid:
- Floating or drifting elements
- Constant background animation
- Parallax scrolling effects
- Page transitions that delay content visibility
- Motion used to add drama to CTAs — cheapens the premium feel
- Flashy entrance effects that distract from the founder and proof content

## 10. Do / don't rules

### Do

1. Put Trần Thủy Tiên's face and voice above the fold on every entry point — she is the trust asset
2. Use specific numbers in every testimonial: name, location, kg/pounds lost, timeframe
3. State the "until goal is reached" guarantee in the headline or subline of the hero — it is the single most powerful differentiator
4. Use the script calligraphy accent for emotional emphasis phrases only — maximum once or twice per section
5. Ensure the consultation CTA appears at least 3 times on the homepage: hero, mid-page proof section, and footer
6. Design a placeholder credential strip/module that can be activated if credentials are confirmed post-meeting
7. Keep section backgrounds in warm cream/ivory family — reserve pure white only for specific high-contrast moments if needed
8. Use warm gold as the primary decorative and structural color — logo, CTA buttons, section dividers, icon accents
9. Make testimonials prominent, named, and specific — they carry more trust weight than any copy
10. Keep body copy line length at 60–70 characters — readable, not overwhelming

### Don't

1. Use before/after gym-style imagery or comparison grids
2. Design with a "weight loss" template feel — no calorie-counter UI, no progress bars, no body weight visualizations
3. Use cold whites or clinical blues anywhere in the palette
4. Place multiple competing CTAs in the same viewport
5. Use countdown timers styled as dashboard widgets — if urgency is needed, use editorial text-based announcement bars only
6. Show pricing or discount information in a promotional banner style — it breaks the premium feel
7. Use stock photography for any testimonial section — only real client photos or named text-only testimonials
8. Animate proof content or guarantee statements — these must be immediately and fully readable
9. Use icon-heavy section headers — the brand communicates through words and photography, not illustration
10. Design a menu that requires more than one click to reach the consultation booking — direct access to CTA at all times
