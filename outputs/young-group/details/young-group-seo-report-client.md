## Your SEO Score: 29 / 100

Think of this like a health check for your website. A score of 29 means the site is alive and working — but Google and other search engines can barely find you right now.

The good news: your brand is strong. You have a real audience, great testimonials, a published book, and active social media. The problems are mostly technical and fixable. Several of the most impactful changes take less than an hour.

## Why Is the Score Low?

There are two main reasons.

**Reason 1 — Google cannot read your website.**
Your site is built on LadiPage, which is excellent for Facebook ads and paid campaigns. But when Google tries to crawl your site, it sees almost nothing — no photos, no text, no descriptions. Everything on your page is loaded by a small program that runs in the browser, and Google's crawler doesn't wait for it. From Google's perspective, your homepage is essentially blank.

**Reason 2 — The basics are missing.**
Your page title says "tranthuytien." Your page description says "tranthuytien." There are no ratings, no business information in a format Google understands, and no proof of who you are as a professional. These are the first things Google checks, and right now they are all empty.

You have a 237,000-follower Facebook page that shows up higher in search than your own website when people search your name. That's how invisible the website currently is to search engines.

## Area 1 — First Impressions (On-Page Basics)

**Score: 18 / 100**

When someone searches for you on Google, the first thing they see is your page title and description. Right now both say "tranthuytien" — which tells potential clients nothing and tells Google nothing.

When someone shares your website link on Facebook or Zalo, there is no preview image. The link shows up as a blank box. For a social-first business, this is a daily missed opportunity.

### What to fix

- [ ] Write a proper page title that describes what you do (example: *Trần Thủy Tiên | Personalized Weight Loss Coaching for Vietnamese in the US*)
- [ ] Write a page description that gives people a reason to click (example: *1-on-1 coaching with personalized meal plans and daily support. Clients across the US, Australia, and Canada.*)
- [ ] Add a branded photo (1200×630px) so your website link shows a real image when shared on Facebook, Zalo, or messages
- [ ] Fix the main headline on your page so it registers as the most important heading — right now Google sees all your headings as equal, so it cannot tell what your page is actually about

## Area 2 — Trust and Credibility

**Score: 31 / 100**

Google holds health and weight loss websites to a very high standard. It wants to see that the person giving advice is qualified, that clients are real, and that the business is legitimate. This category is called E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness).

Your coaching story is compelling. The problem is that none of it is presented in a way Google can verify — or that first-time visitors can quickly confirm.

There are also two legal risks worth addressing now:

**Medical claims in testimonials.** Some testimonials mention diabetes, blood pressure, and a cancer patient. In the US, Australia, Canada, UK, and Germany, health claims in advertising require a disclaimer stating results are not typical and the program is not a substitute for medical advice. Without this, there is regulatory exposure in every market you serve.

**Privacy policy.** You collect names, phone numbers, and emails through your contact form. Every country you target (US, EU/Germany, Australia, Canada, UK) requires a privacy policy explaining how you use that data. This is not optional in those markets.

### What to fix

- [ ] Add a short medical disclaimer near the testimonials section (*"Results are not typical and individual results vary. This program is not a substitute for medical advice."*)
- [ ] Create a privacy policy page (a basic template is enough to start)
- [ ] Add a credentials section to your page listing your training background, certifications, or any professional affiliations
- [ ] Link your book *Béo ơi vĩnh biệt* to a place where people can find or buy it (Amazon, a publisher page, or a Google Books listing)
- [ ] Remove or replace the countdown timer showing 00:00:00 — a timer stuck at zero reads as a fake urgency tactic and hurts trust
- [ ] Add dates to your testimonials so visitors can see how recent the results are

## Area 3 — Local and Business Presence

**Score: 18 / 100**

You have a real US address in Texas. That means you qualify for a Google Business Profile — the listing that shows up on Google Maps and in the local results when people search for coaches near them. Right now, no Google Business Profile is linked to your website.

Creating one is free and takes about an hour. It is one of the highest-impact things you can do for local search visibility.

There are also some small inconsistencies in how your business name and address appear on the page. Consistent formatting matters when Google and directories are comparing your information.

### What to fix

- [ ] Create a Google Business Profile for your US address (Midlothian, TX) — category: "Weight Loss Coach" or "Health Coach"
- [ ] Create a second Google Business Profile for your Vietnam address (Quận 6, HCMC)
- [ ] Fix the address format on your page — it currently shows `Midlothian. TX76065` (period instead of comma). Should be `Midlothian, TX 76065`
- [ ] Pick one spelling of your name and use it everywhere: either `Trần Thủy Tiên` or `Trần Thuỷ Tiên` — both versions currently appear on your page
- [ ] Ask past US-based clients to leave a Google review on your new Business Profile
- [ ] Install Google Analytics so you can see how many people visit your website and where they come from

## Area 4 — How Google Reads Your Business Information

**Score: 0 / 100**

There is a system called "structured data" (also called Schema) that lets you give Google specific, organized facts about your business — your name, address, phone number, services, and client reviews — in a format Google can reliably read. Right now your site has none of this.

Adding it is like handing Google a completed profile card instead of making it guess from reading your page.

### What to fix

- [ ] Add your business information in structured format (name, address, phone, services) — your web developer or LadiPage can inject this into the page's code
- [ ] Add your professional profile (name, job title, book, social media links) in structured format
- [ ] Once you have Google reviews, add a star rating display to your page

---

## Area 5 — Website Speed and Performance

**Estimated status: Poor**

Your website takes over 4 seconds to fully load on a typical mobile connection. Google counts page speed as a ranking factor, and slow pages also lose visitors — most people leave if a page takes longer than 3 seconds.

The main causes are platform-related (LadiPage loads everything through JavaScript, which takes time), but there are practical improvements that do not require rebuilding the site.

### What to fix

- [ ] Put your website behind Cloudflare (free) — this alone would cut your load time significantly by caching your site closer to your visitors
- [ ] Confirm that your hosting has compression turned on (gzip or brotli) — this reduces the amount of data sent to visitors' browsers
- [ ] Fix the four expired countdown timers — each one adds unnecessary code that slows the page
- [ ] Remove the two outdated browser scripts your page loads (`html5shiv` and `respond`) — these were written for Internet Explorer 8 and serve no purpose for any of your current visitors

## Area 6 — AI Search Visibility

**Score: 20 / 100**

AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI answers are becoming how many people find services. They work differently from traditional search — they read your site, extract facts, and summarize them. If your site cannot be read (because everything is in JavaScript), AI tools cannot cite you.

Your YouTube channel is your strongest asset here. Research shows YouTube is highly correlated with AI citations. You have 14 videos linked from your site. The opportunity is to make that content available as readable text.

### What to fix

- [ ] Create an `llms.txt` file — a simple text file on your website that tells AI systems who you are and what you do (similar to a bio card for AI tools)
- [ ] Publish written summaries or transcripts of your top 5 YouTube videos on your website — this makes your expertise readable to AI systems
- [ ] Write a clear "about" section that describes your methodology in plain language — AI tools need self-contained paragraphs they can quote

## Area 7 — Content for Search (Long-Term Growth)

This is not a quick fix — it is the strategic growth opportunity.

Right now, when someone searches for "giảm cân cho người Việt ở Mỹ" or "Vietnamese weight loss coach," your website does not appear. Google shows hospital articles, health magazines, and YouTube videos in those results. A sales page alone cannot rank for these queries.

The way to build organic search traffic over time is to publish articles that answer the questions your audience is already asking — then those articles lead people to your program.

### What to build

- [ ] Add a blog or resources section to your website (even 4–6 articles is a strong start)
- [ ] Write an article targeting: *Why Vietnamese people gain weight after moving to the US*
- [ ] Write an article targeting: *How to lose weight eating Vietnamese food in America*
- [ ] Write an article targeting: *Weight loss options for Vietnamese women over 40*
- [ ] Create a dedicated page for the Vietnamese community in Southern California (Garden Grove / Little Saigon) — the largest Vietnamese diaspora community in the US
- [ ] Create a Frequently Asked Questions section on your homepage answering the top 5–8 questions your clients ask before they sign up

## A Note on LadiPage

LadiPage is a strong tool for the business you are running today — paid Facebook ads, Zalo campaigns, and warm social referrals. It converts well for that audience.

For organic search growth, it has a structural limitation: Google cannot read the content it produces. This does not mean you need to abandon LadiPage immediately. It means that if organic search becomes a growth priority, a two-track approach makes sense — keep LadiPage for paid traffic, and build a blog or resource site on a search-friendly platform for organic.

The recommendations above are ordered so that the early phases deliver real value even within the current LadiPage setup. The platform migration is a later, strategic decision — not an emergency.
