# Visual Strategist Decision Brief

## Purpose

This document is the final human-reviewed decision brief between Business Persona and Branding Visual Guide generation.

Its job is to:

* convert business and conversion context into visual decisions
* resolve ambiguity through human-in-the-loop questions
* lock enough direction that the final Branding Visual Guide can be generated consistently

This is not the final client guide.
This is the decision input for generating the final guide.

## 1. Input summary from Business Persona

### Business

* Brand name: Young Group / Trần Thủy Tiên
* Category: Online weight loss coaching — Vietnamese diaspora market
* Offer: Online weight loss program with 42-meal food combo; personalized meal plans by body type; ~15-min lunch/dinner prep; clients do not go hungry; double money-back guarantee
* Service area: Vietnam, USA, Australia, Canada, Germany, UK
* Primary CTA: Book consultation or enroll — exact label TBD
* Sales model: Social media + referral program ($50 per successful referral)
* Trust burden: High — double guarantee language on a single page; hyper-specific testimonials with exact weight numbers; no third-party review platform presence compounds the gap
* Buyer urgency: High — countdown timer on 50% discount; medical-urgency testimonials (diabetes, blood pressure, high blood lipids resolved)

### Audience

* Primary audience: Vietnamese women 30–60, USA primary
* Secondary audience: Vietnam, Australia, Canada, Germany, UK
* Buyer trigger: Failed past weight loss attempts after years of trying; frustration with complicated, restrictive programs
* Main objection: "Will this be complicated? Will I have to go hungry?"
* Proof needed: Specific client count with success rate; named testimonials with weight numbers, locations, and time frames; 226K Facebook followers prominently featured; money-back guarantee placed earlier in page flow

### Brand

* Desired feeling: Trusted, Simple, Achievable, Understood
* Core traits: Direct and specific; warm and culturally grounded; confident in results; simple-first; empathetic to failure history
* Anti-traits: Vague or adjective-heavy; clinical or medical in tone; English-first framing; aspirational without proof; effort-forward
* Voice cues: Lead with outcome; exact numbers; pair every claim with a name, number, or guarantee; short sentences; "bạn" (you) framing
* Messaging cues: "[X pounds] in [timeframe] — [client name], [US state]"; "No starving. No hunger."; "Simple - Easy - Effective"; "Full refund if no result"

### Strategic notes

* What the website must optimize for: Booking a consultation or enrollment — single conversion goal. Trust must be earned fast; this audience has been burned before.
* What the brand must NOT feel like: Generic English wellness brand; clinical health service; aspirational lifestyle coaching; any program that signals "hard work ahead"
* Any unresolved business questions that affect visuals: Client count and founding year are unknown — these directly affect proof section design and prominence. Brand colors, typography direction, and logo style are now confirmed from visual assets.

## 2. Visual direction recommendation

### Primary direction

Enterprise / Stable

### Secondary influence

Editorial / Human

### Direction mix

* Primary %: 65%
* Secondary %: 35%

### Why this direction

* Trust burden is the dominant constraint. The audience has tried many programs and failed. Enterprise/Stable signals credibility, stability, and "this is real" — the visual equivalent of a guarantee.
* The double money-back guarantee and hyper-specific testimonials are Enterprise/trust signals. The visual system must match the proof model.
* Editorial/Human is the secondary because the brand is founder-led and personal. The caregiver archetype needs warmth and cultural groundedness to soften the authority into accessibility.
* Cutting-edge/Innovative would directly contradict the "Simple, Easy, Effective" positioning. Novelty signals complexity for a buyer who has already been overwhelmed.
* Price point (~$699, post-consultation reveal) requires premium credibility without corporate coldness.
* Anti-traits confirm: not aspirational, not clinical, not generic. Enterprise/Stable avoids aspirational; Editorial/Human avoids clinical and generic.

### Must avoid

1. Generic English wellness aesthetic — minty green, pure white, Helvetica, stock women holding green smoothies (this is what every failed program before Young Group looks like)
2. Clinical blue-and-white health service palette — signals medical authority, not personal coaching; distances from the warm Vietnamese cultural context
3. MLM-style weight loss marketing — neon accents, aggressive countdown banners, anonymous before/after collage grids (trust-destroying category association)
4. Aspirational lifestyle editorial — oversized white space, moody fashion photography, abstract wellness mood shots (aspirational without proof is the anti-pattern the entire brand is built against)
5. SaaS dashboard UI — card metrics, progress widgets, feature comparison tables (signals complexity; the buyer trigger is fleeing programs that felt like too much work)
6. Hyper-feminine beauty brand aesthetic — pastel pinks, script fonts, ribbon and floral decorative elements (too soft, undermines proof-forward credibility)
7. Generic coaching brand defaults — orange motivational accents, "transform your life" headline treatment, stock success portrait photography (indistinguishable from competitors)

## 4. Color direction

Confirmed from visual assets (yg-vibe.jpeg, yg-vibe3.jpeg, yg-vibe4.jpeg). The brand palette is established and consistent across all primary-register assets.

### Primary color logic

* Confirmed anchor color family: Deep espresso brown / warm dark chocolate
* Why: All primary-register assets use deep espresso-brown as the authority anchor. It signals premium credibility and warm authority — not clinical, not corporate, not generic wellness. Confirmed directly from the YG logo mark, display headline treatment, and background fields in the canonical brand asset.
* Domain signal: No other Vietnamese weight loss coaching brand operates in this color register. It clearly differentiates from the green-and-pink social-content tier used by this category.
* Emotional role: Warm authority — "this person knows her craft and has earned trust"

### Base color logic

* Base: Light
* Neutral temperature: Warm — ivory/champagne base, not pure white
* Contrast level: High — deep espresso on warm ivory creates strong contrast; conversion-critical for proof elements and CTAs

### Accent logic

* Accent usage: Controlled
* Accent role: Logo mark, CTA highlights, proof element emphasis
* Accent: Bronze-gold — used on the YG logo mark and select headline accents in the primary brand register. Signals premium quality without becoming decorative noise.
* Accent caution: Gold is a mark-level accent, not a UI fill. Never use it on large surfaces, form fields, or background fills.

### Palette — Locked from assets

* Base: Warm ivory / champagne — `#F5EFE4` approximate (soft, not pure white)
* Neutral secondary: Warm stone / taupe — `#D9CDBF` approximate (alternating section background)
* Anchor: Deep espresso brown — `#3B2314` approximate (primary headings, borders, authority elements)
* Accent: Bronze-gold — `#B8903A` approximate (logo mark, CTA highlights, proof element emphasis)
* Text primary: Deep espresso brown or near-black warm — `#2C1A0E` approximate

Note: Hex values are approximated from visual inspection of assets. Final hex values must be confirmed from the original brand kit or extracted via design tool from the provided assets.

### Usage ratio

* Neutrals (ivory + stone): 65–70%
* Anchor (espresso): 20–25%
* Accent (bronze-gold): 5%

### State color rule

* Default state colors acceptable: No
* If no: Default browser blue is incompatible with this warm brown-and-ivory system. All interactive states (hover, focus, active, disabled) must use warm-system colors — espresso darken/lighten on hover, warm stone for disabled, gold ring for focus.

### Color anti-patterns

* Pure white backgrounds — breaks the warm ivory register established across all assets
* Minty greens or medical blues — the brand's social-content green tier (vibe2) must not leak into the website's primary brand register
* Hot pink or neon — MLM category association; appears in social content only, must never enter the conversion page
* Multiple competing accents — only one accent (bronze-gold); adding red, blue, or secondary accent destroys the premium register
* Dark mode backgrounds — the brand is light-and-warm; full-dark inverts the trust signal

## 5. Typography direction

Confirmed from visual assets. The type system is visible and consistent across primary-register assets.

### Typeface strategy

Hybrid — High-contrast display serif primary for headlines, humanist sans-serif for body and UI, script reserved for founder signature only

### Named fonts — Locked from assets

* Display / headline font: **Playfair Display** — the letterforms in yg-vibe.jpeg match Playfair Display exactly (high-contrast stroke, transitional serif, elegant cap forms). Confirmed role: hero headlines, section leads, guarantee callouts. Vietnamese diacritic support: confirmed via Google Fonts coverage.
* Body / UI font: **Be Vietnam Pro** — the warmest, cleanest humanist sans with first-class Vietnamese diacritic support. Used for body copy, proof chips, navigation, form labels, and all secondary text. Confirmed role: legibility system-wide.
* Script: **Great Vibes** or **Dancing Script** — used exclusively for the founder name signature (as seen in yg-vibe.jpeg, bottom-right: "Trần Thủy Tiên"). Must not be used for any other element. Vietnamese diacritics render adequately at signature scale.

### Typography role split

* Display (Playfair Display): Hero headlines, section leads, guarantee callouts, proof section kickers
* Body (Be Vietnam Pro): All running copy, proof chips, navigation, form labels, CTA button labels, testimonials
* Script (Great Vibes or Dancing Script): Founder signature only — one instance per layout context
* Approx usage ratio: 65% sans body / 30% display serif / 5% script

### Tone

Human / Warm

### Density

Balanced

### Hierarchy

Strong — Playfair Display at large display sizes creates immediate authority; Be Vietnam Pro at body creates reading comfort

### Heading behavior

* Sentence case
* Normal tracking for Playfair Display (never tighten aggressively; it breaks the letterform)
* Calm — authority from the typeface's inherent contrast, not from weight or caps pressure

### Body behavior

* Plain
* Reading tone: Direct, warm
* Default weight: Regular (400) or Medium (500) — Be Vietnam Pro light weights render poorly for Vietnamese diacritics at body sizes; never go below 400

### Signature move

Playfair Display italic for founder-voice moments — quote leads, guarantee statements, one personal kicker per section. The italic form amplifies warmth and personal authority simultaneously.

### Typography anti-patterns

* Script used for any element other than founder signature — diacritic rendering degrades at small sizes; overuse collapses the premium register
* Playfair Display at body sizes — too low x-height for Vietnamese at 16px and below; reserve for 24px and above
* Be Vietnam Pro at display sizes without sufficient weight — too neutral to carry authority in hero contexts
* All-caps Vietnamese headlines — diacritics stack awkwardly; creates visual chaos, not emphasis
* Generic SaaS pairing (Inter + any geometric sans) — signals tech product, destroys the warm authority register

## 6. Layout rhythm

### Layout mode

Flow-based

### Grid

Standard (12-column base — consistent, legible, not experimental)

### Rhythm

Alternating — warm off-white and warm stone/taupe sections alternate to create breathing room and guide scroll

### Density

Balanced

### Section pacing

Narrative — problem → solution → proof → guarantee → CTA

### Hero structure

Text + portrait — founder's face is the primary trust signal; opens with founder image + outcome-first headline

### Common section patterns

1. Full-width narrative (hero — problem acknowledgment + outcome promise)
2. Proof band (testimonial cluster — named, numbered, located)
3. 2-column (program feature + proof or visual — benefit left, evidence right)
4. Founder block (personal voice + guarantee statement)
5. CTA band (consultation booking — repeated at 2–3 strategic points in the page)

### Mobile behavior

* What must remain true on mobile: Proof numbers stay large and legible; CTA remains thumb-reachable and always visible; testimonial names and locations do not truncate; guarantee language stays visible above the fold
* What may simplify on mobile: 2-column sections collapse to single-column; section background alternation simplifies

### Layout anti-patterns

* Dashboard feel — SaaS card grid, feature matrix, progress indicators
* Hyper-asymmetric editorial grid — trust-breaking for a coaching conversion page
* Long unbroken copy walls — Vietnamese women scanning on mobile will abandon
* Multiple competing CTAs within the same viewport section
* Hero section without founder face — the brand's trust model is founder-led

## 7. Component language

### Overall feel

Soft / Rounded

### Card language

Shadow-led — warm depth; flat cards read as cold for this category

### Button language

* Filled
* Rounded rectangle
* Premium — CTA communicates "ready when you are," not "BUY NOW"

### Form language

High-trust — consultation form must feel secure and personal; warm field styling, clear Vietnamese labels, no chrome excess

### Navigation feel

Quiet / Direct — single conversion page; navigation must not compete with the conversion flow

### Proof modules

1. Testimonial cards (named, numbered, located — the brand's core proof mechanism)
2. Results chips (weight + timeframe stamps — compact proof for scanning)
3. Trust banner (226K followers + guarantee claim + service area)
4. Founder note (personal voice, guarantee context, tone-setter)
5. Credential bar (book authorship, platform reach, six-country service area)

### Signature component

**Proof chip cluster** — named client testimonials rendered as compact cards: "[Name], [US State] — [X lbs in Y weeks]." Groups of 3–4 per cluster, warm card surface, anchor-color name label, subdued body text. This directly visualizes the brand's specific-proof-over-generic-claim model and cannot be mistaken for stock content.

### Component anti-patterns

* App-style UI chrome — tab bars, toggles, step-indicators, metric widgets
* MLM-style before/after collages — trust-breaking; strong category contamination
* Generic card grids with decorative icons — looks like a feature-list SaaS, not a results coaching brand
* Multiple CTA button styles competing — dilutes the primary action signal
* Oversized decorative illustration — this brand's iconography is proof data, not artwork

## 9. Motion and interaction

### Motion level

Restrained

### Motion role

Reveal / Hover

### Button interaction

Tactile — subtle fill shift or scale on hover; premium, not flashy

### Scroll behavior

Fade-up — section reveals as user scrolls; guides attention without performing

### Performance rule

* What should animate: Section fade-up reveals; button hover states; testimonial card entrance on scroll
* What should never animate: Proof numbers and metrics (animated counters feel inflated and erode trust); any element that could appear fabricated in motion

### Reduced motion rule

Required: Yes — prefers-reduced-motion must be respected; primary audience includes users 50–60 for whom heavy motion is disorienting

### Motion anti-patterns

* Animated countdown timer beyond what already exists — additional timer motion signals pressure, not trust
* Parallax background effects — complex motion contradicts "Simple, Easy" positioning
* Floating persistent widgets in motion
* Transition effects that delay reading access to proof content

## 10. Do / don't rules

### Do

1. Lead every section with an outcome or proof — never with a feature description
2. Use the founder's face prominently in the hero and personal-voice sections
3. Show exact numbers (weight lost, client count, follower count) — round numbers read as invented
4. Repeat the guarantee in at least two distinct locations in the page flow
5. Render testimonials as named proof chips: [Name], [State], [X lbs in Y weeks]
6. Use warm ivory base and deep espresso brown to signal credibility without clinical coldness; bronze-gold for mark-level accents only
7. Keep CTAs to one primary action per viewport section
8. Render Vietnamese text at sufficient size and weight for clean diacritic display — never light weight at small sizes
9. Make the 226K Facebook follower count visually prominent — treat it as a trust anchor, not footer detail
10. Ensure CTA, guarantee, and a proof element are visible above the fold on mobile

### Don't

1. Use generic wellness stock photography (women on scales, green smoothies, measuring tape, workout gear)
2. Use blue-and-white or green-and-white as anchor palette — these are clinical and generic wellness signals
3. Show before/after collages in the transformation or MLM style
4. Lead with program features before proof and outcomes
5. Use condensed or aggressive headings — authority comes from data, not typographic pressure
6. Animate proof numbers or testimonial metrics
7. Build multi-page navigation if the conversion goal is single consultation or enrollment
8. Use English-first layout conventions (English CTA labels, English-centric imagery) — the audience is Vietnamese
9. Use bright pastels or hyper-feminine accents — the brand is warm, not sweet
10. Introduce more than one signature typographic or component pattern per section
